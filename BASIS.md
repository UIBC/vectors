A citation that is not there fails the gate. A `none` check that cites something also fails: "we invented it" and "somebody required it" cannot both be true of one rule.

**8 of the citations are individual Internet-Drafts (S10, S17, S18, S19, S2, S3, S8, S9).** A draft is a proposal by named authors, not a consensus standard: "direct" here means the draft text states the requirement, and it does not mean a published standard does. That distinction is the difference between citing a proposal and citing a rule, and it is the one a reader should check before quoting a count.

## What this table does not say

It does not say the `direct` checks are implemented correctly - that is what the corpus and the adversarial cases are for. It does not say the `none` checks are wrong to exist: a format needs behaviour no draft has specified yet, and inventing it openly is better than attributing it to a document that does not say it. It says only which of the two happened, per rule.
